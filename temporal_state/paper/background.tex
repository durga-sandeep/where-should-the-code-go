\paragraph{Temporal state for conditional simulation.}
A simulation initialized from operational reports must distinguish when an event
occurred from when its report became available. A receipt can arrive before its
record, and a later correction can revise an earlier quantity. Reconstructing
inventory at a historical time therefore requires both a reporting cutoff and
event-order replay. In lost-sales dynamics, demand consumes available stock and
unmet demand is lost; exchanging a receipt and a demand event can change the
trajectory even when their totals agree. We study this reconstruction step and
its consequences under supplied future demand and receipt scenarios. These are
conditional rollouts, rather than predictions of unknown demand or an ordering
policy optimization task. Holding future inputs fixed separates errors in the
starting state from uncertainty about future inputs.

\paragraph{Relation to existing evaluations.}
TimeBench evaluates broad temporal reasoning~\citep{chu2024timebench}; ETRQA
examines event-time constraints and comparisons, including compound
questions~\citep{luo2025etrqa}; and TimE extends evaluation across factual,
news and dialog contexts~\citep{wei2025time}. These establish that ordering,
temporal filtering and evolving narratives are existing evaluation topics.
More directly, the public BiTempQA dataset card describes separate event and
record timestamps, late information, version tracking and knowledge
retraction~\citep{bitempqa}. We therefore make no claim to introduce bitemporal
question answering. TemporalBench studies historical interpretation, forecasting,
contextual reasoning and event-conditioned prediction on numerical time
series~\citep{weng2026temporalbench}. Our emphasis is the effect of report
availability and revision resolution on a replayed state and its subsequent
conditional trajectory. InventoryBench evaluates inventory-control agents under
demand shifts and uncertain lead times, with on-hand inventory supplied to the
decision maker~\citep{baek2026inventory}. STOCKTAKE separately measures stated
beliefs and control in partially observed supply chains~\citep{deb2026stocktake}.
Our narrower target is exact reconstruction from visible reports followed by a
fixed-schedule calculation, without hidden-state inference or policy choice.
Delayed reporting is distinct from delayed physical delivery. FreshRetailNet supplies hourly
sales and stockout annotations for demand-recovery research~\citep{wang2025freshretailnet};
using its observed profiles does not validate our synthetic reporting process.
We test which errors survive executable report selection and state preparation,
and whether exact continuation corrects or exposes stockout errors inherited from
model-emitted state. The contribution is this controlled empirical diagnosis,
not the general distinction between state estimation and downstream computation.
