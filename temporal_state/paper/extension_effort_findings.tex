Under the forced answer call used in the original grid, high effort changes mini's canonical-input future-joint accuracy by -41.1 [-51.0, -31.2] points on synthetic histories and -17.7 [-30.2, -5.2] on profiles, raw-input accuracy by -12.5 [-21.9, -3.1] and +8.3 [-1.0, +17.7], and snapshot accuracy by +7.8 [+3.1, +12.5] and +4.2 [-4.2, +12.5] (GPT-5.4: Table~\ref{tab:effort}); its wrong canonical stocks are mostly exactly zero, and re-issued reasoning summaries show it planning the replay yet answering first (Appendix~\ref{app:extensions}). Against the same-period replication baseline, whose run-to-run shift sets a noise floor near nine points (Appendix~\ref{app:extensions}), the canonical drop is still -32.3 [-42.2, -22.4]. The decisive control removes the forced call: with automatic tool choice, mini at low and high effort reaches 86.5 and 93.2\% on canonical input (+6.8 [+0.5, +13.5]) and 56.8 and 79.2\% on raw input (+22.4 [+14.1, +30.7]), with 2 of 768 calls returning no answer. The collapse is therefore an interaction between the forced answer call and requested effort rather than an effect of requested effort alone; the design cannot separate removing the forced call from permitting free text before the answer, and the canonical gain under automatic choice is within the noise floor while the raw gain is not. Automatic choice alone lifts mini's low-effort accuracy from 37.5 to 56.8\% (raw) and 76.0 to 86.5\% (canonical), so the forced-call mini cells elsewhere are pessimistic by 10 to 19 points.
